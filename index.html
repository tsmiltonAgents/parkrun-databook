A data book

parkrun, by the numbers

How a free Saturday 5K in a London park became a global ritual — and what nearly a million finishes a week reveal about people, places and time.
Built from public parkrun results — the complete history of Bushy parkrun (≈973,000 finishes) and a worldwide census of 2,357 events — with weather from Open-Meteo. Every claim was red-teamed; every figure regenerates from source.

The findings, in one breath

  1. How a park run conquered the world — from 13 runners in 2004 to ~2,900 events in 21 countries, spread by language and migration rather than geography.
  2. The fun run is secretly arithmetic — a 48% clock-pull just under 20 minutes; the fastest finisher frozen for 14 years while the field grew 100-fold.
  3. Who keeps showing up — 60% never return; the milestone t-shirt bends the quit curve; clubs recruit the already-fast.
  4. Is parkrun the same everywhere? — age-grade medians span 18 points; turnout mirrors across the hemispheres; rain empties the start line.
  5. The planet hits pause — every country fell to zero in one week of March 2020, then reopened across nineteen months.
  6. The fragile scaffolding — the whole edifice rests on a thin, unpaid, sublinear volunteer core.

A Saturday in a London park

On 2 October 2004, thirteen people ran a timed 5K around Bushy Park in Teddington. Twenty years later the same free, volunteer-run ritual happens every Saturday at ~2,900 events in 21 countries. The data reveals something more interesting than a running boom: a global human habit governed by psychology, survivorship, climate and culture far more than by fitness.

How this was made — data, maths & stats

Data. All 2,922 parkrun events from events.json (location and name). Launch year extracted from the earliest date in 409 per-event history files (histories/*.csv), matched by countrycode + eventname. Coverage: 411 events sampled (~14.1%); 2,511 events have no history file yet (unsampled). The cumulative panel includes only countries with ≥13 sampled launches.

Method. Each event is plotted as a dot on a world map; unsampled events (grey) show 86% of the dataset are not yet dated. Coloured dots (sampled) are tinted by launch year (2004–2026), drawn in ascending order so older launches appear beneath newer ones. The second panel counts cumulative launches over time per country using only the sampled launch dates, drawn as step lines.

Maths & stats. For each (cc, slug) pair, the minimum date is extracted: launch_year = min(date) across all rows in the history file. Cumulative counts are exact tallies of the sorted sample dates per country. No statistical model or estimation—the lines show the empirical step function of sampled entry timing.

Robustness. The key caveat is sampling: histories cover only ~14% of events, so colours and launch dates are biased toward early events (which are more likely archived). The cumulative-launch lines show country order and pattern reliably (UK first, others in waves) but are not absolute event totals—UK's true count exceeds 1,200, while the sample shows 146. Lines are labelled with sample size (n) to make this transparent.

How this was made — data, maths & stats

Data. 407 event weekly-finisher histories from parkrun's API, sampled across 12 countries. Cohort = 143 "well-established" events launched on or before 2017-06-01 with ≥100 weeks recorded. UK-heavy (57%), with median observed life of 635 weeks (~12 years). Bucketed into integer week-bins by weeks-since-launch; used only weeks with ≥40% event coverage (out to week 520, ~10 years).

Method. Each event's weekly finisher counts were sorted by date and assigned weeks-since-launch as (date − launch_date).days / 7. Finishers per integer week-bin were averaged across multiple observations. For the cohort, we computed the 10th, 25th, 50th, 75th, and 90th percentiles of finishers at each week across all events. The median trajectory was lightly smoothed (7-point rolling median) to suppress weather/holiday noise while preserving the shape.

Maths & stats. Logistic fit to smoothed cohort median via grid search + local refinement minimising SSE: F(t) = K / (1 + exp(−r·(t − t₀))), yielding K = 163 finishers/wk (carrying capacity), r = 0.0162/wk (growth rate), t₀ = 47.2 wk (inflection), R² = 0.942. Time to 90% of K computed as t₀ + ln(9)/r ≈ 183 weeks (~3.5 years).

Robustness. COVID-19 caused a near-total pause (2020-03 to 2021-07), visible as a single notch in the calendar-aligned median; since-launch axis blurs this interruption. The logistic fit was applied to the smooth-rising, pre-plateau portion and holds across all three exemplar events (14× size range), validating that saturation is shape-driven, not confounded by sampling or event selection.

How this was made — data, maths & stats

Data. From 2,357 parkrun event histories (weekly records, lon/lat, ~2,134 events with ≥52 weeks of activity across all countries). Nearest-neighbour distance computed among the full 2,922-event census; final analysis n=2,154 events binned by count of other parkruns within 25 km.

Method. For each event, count how many other parkruns sit within a 25 km radius using haversine distance between coordinates. Bin events into six isolation categories (0, 1–2, 3–5, 6–11, 12–24, 25+ neighbours) and compute the mean weekly finisher count for each bin. Repeat the correlation analysis within each country separately, then pool the within-country correlations to isolate the isolation–size relationship from country-level confounds (e.g. UK's size and clustering).

Maths & stats. Bin means computed as simple arithmetic mean of size; error bars are 1.96 × SEM (95% confidence interval). Pearson correlation between log(isolation distance) and log(size) within country, then pooled: r = −0.49 (range −0.29 to −0.61 per country). Monotonic increase: 53 → 78 → 116 → 170 → 193 → 218 finishers (4.1× effect). Dropping the UK's 884 events alone reproduces the ladder (52→211), confirming it is not a UK-composition artefact.

Robustness. Event maturity controlled: restricting to ≥52 weeks leaves correlation unchanged. Medians track means (40→189), ruling out heavy-tail skew. Within-country demeaning removes country composition as confound. Not causal—this is culture and population density correlation, not proof that proximity growth drives attendance.

How this was made — data, maths & stats

Data. 2,357 parkrun event histories (country code, weekly finisher counts). Filtered to 2,273 events with ≥20 weeks of data to ensure stable mean estimates. No individual names/times. All events are 5k series only; junior 2k events excluded from source. Sample covers 12 countries: UK (n=605), Australia (346), South Africa (140), New Zealand (64), Ireland (49), Netherlands (32), Poland (31), Germany (30), Canada (29), USA (28), Japan (22), Italy (20).

Method. Computed mean weekly finishers per event using all available weeks. Tested global distribution by fitting a normal curve to log(mean finishers). For the between/within decomposition, grouped events by country, computed the mean of log(mean) per country, then decomposed the total variance in log-size into between-country and within-country components using sum-of-squares.

Maths & stats. Lognormal fit checked via Kolmogorov–Smirnov test: KS = max|F_emp − F_normal| where F is the cumulative distribution of standardized log-size. Result: KS = 0.035, indicating lognormal shape (vs. KS = 0.146 for raw scale). Variance decomposition: between-country share = Σ n_c(μ_c − μ_global)² / [Σ n_c(μ_c − μ_global)² + Σ_c Σ(log-size_i − μ_c)²] = 38%. Log-space statistics: mean 4.53, SD 0.89, skewness −0.18 (symmetric).

Robustness. KS statistic stable across minimum-weeks thresholds (10, 20, 50, 100 weeks all yield D ≈ 0.034–0.035KS = 0.028 (better fit), UK-only KS = 0.032. Recent 52-week window shows same symmetry (log-skew −0.22). Power-law alternative explicitly tested via Hill estimator; alpha drifts 4.8→3.3 across cutoffs — no stable heavy tail.

The fun run is secretly arithmetic

How this was made — data, maths & stats

Data. 914,037 finish times from all 1,093 Bushy Park parkrun events (Oct 2004–Jun 2026). Dropped athlete_id 2214 (placeholder) and blank times. Analysis window 15:00–45:00 min contains 888,690 finishes. Split by speed band: sub-20 min (n=58,054), 20–25 (n=274,691), 25–30 (n=310,570), 30–40 (n=221,662).

Method. Built a 1-second histogram of all finish times. For each of the 30 round minutes (20:00, 25:00, etc.), fitted a smooth baseline count using a wide Gaussian smoother (bandwidth 25 s) on seconds outside the heap zone (masked ±15 s around each round minute, plus half-minutes). Then compared observed counts just-below (M−15 to M−1 s) versus just-above (M+1 to M+15 s) the round minute, relative to baseline. Within each speed band, refined the baseline to a local-linear fit on the flanks (M−30 to M−14 and M+14 to M+30) to remove density slope bias.

Maths & stats. Excess ratio = observed / expected baseline. Aggregated across offsets 1–13 s and 29 round minutes via paired sign test: share-below = 194,516 / (194,516 + 187,233) = 0.5095, 95% CI [0.5080, 0.5111], z = 11.79. Per-band step magnitude = (below surplus %) + (above deficit %); sub-20 step = 48.3%, 20–25 step = 22.6%, 25–30 step = 14.2%, 30–40 step = 3.2%.

Robustness. Initial per-band analysis suffered a confound: within a speed band the underlying density has a slope, biasing below vs above even with zero heaping. Fixed by switching to detrended local-linear baseline on the flanks only. Year-by-year analysis shows the effect is stable: weighted regression slope ≈ −0.0002 / yr (effectively zero), rejecting the hypothesis that goal-chasing escalated in the app era.

How this was made — data, maths & stats

Data. All 910,546 valid finishes from 1,093 Bushy parkrun events (2004–2025). Drops: athlete_id 2214 (placeholder), missing/invalid age-groups (SM---, WWC, blank: 4,441 rows), blank or invalid age-grade values (≤0% or >110%: 1,204 rows). Remaining: 972,551 read → 910,546 kept. Each finish is one data point; runners with multiple races counted multiple times. Per-runner view deduplicates: each athlete contributes their median age-grade once per 5-year age band per gender.

Method. Age-groups (e.g. SM25-29, VW70-74) converted to numeric midpoints (e.g. 27, 72). Finishes binned by gender and age. For each bin, computed median age-grade (%) and bootstrap 95% confidence interval (1,000 replicates, seed=0). Per-runner view: for each (gender, age) bin, calculate each athlete's own median grade, then take median of those individual medians (Nuniq >= 50 for per-runner plot to avoid noise). Smoothed using locally weighted regression (LOESS) with tricube weights, span 0.45, quadratic kernel.

Maths & stats. Population median M_pop = median({all finishes in bin}). Per-runner median M_runner = median({athlete_1_median, athlete_2_median, ...}). Bootstrap CI: resample finishes (or runner medians) 1,000 times with replacement, take 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles of bootstrap medians. LOESS: solve weighted least-squares (XTWX)β = XTWy for each smoothing point, where weights are tricube w(u) = (1−|u|3)3 clipped to kernel width. Female age-82 example: pop median 62.5% vs per-runner 56.9%, non-overlapping CIs indicate counting artifact.

Robustness. Key confound ruled out by red-team: the dramatic "women peak in 80s" (62–63%) survives only in population view, driven by repeat-attendance weighting (devoted octogenarians running 11–16 races each vs. younger runners ~3–4 races). Per-runner dedup removes this weighting; the surviving female rebound (trough ~37 → peak ~67) is robust (Nuniq 1,320–3,015). Male veterans plateau holds in both views. Thin-data threshold: ages with N < 150 (population) or Nuniq < 50 (per-runner) marked hollow to flag low credibility.

Data. All Bushy Park bushy_*.csv finishes concatenated. Dropped athlete_id 2214 (placeholder) and records with age_grade ≤ 0. Kept only gender = 'male' or 'female'. Sample: 910,766 finishes (554,275 men, 356,491 women), covering age brackets 25–84.

Method. For each 5-year age bracket (25–29 through 80–84), computed mean age-grade separately by sex. Plotted both curves and found where women's mean crosses men's (Panel A). Separately, calculated the share of finishes scoring age-grade >70% for each sex (Panel B).

Maths & stats. Linear interpolation to find crossover age: for each adjacent pair of brackets where gap[i] < 0 &<= gap[i+1], solved t = −gap[i] / (gap[i+1] − gap[i]) and recovered age as mid[i] + t·(mid[i+1] − mid[i]). Result: crossover ≈ age 63. Elite ratio: (men >70%) / (women >70%) = 12.16% / 7.06% = 1.72×. Late-life gap at 80–84: women +10.1 percentage-points higher mean age-grade.

Robustness. Red-team found repeat-attendance weighting inflates the +10.1pp tip (a few devoted octogenarian women log many runs); deduplicating per-athlete shrinks it to +2.78pp. However, the crossover location (~63–65) and the male elite dominance (ratio 1.72×, even 2.07× per-person) are robust. Crossover present in both early and late eras; not a growth or table-refresh artifact. The 80–84 cohort is small (881 finishes, 81 women) and self-selected.

How this was made — data, maths & stats

Data. Weekly parkrun results from Bushy, 1,093 events over 2004–2026 (11–55 per year). Each row contains finishers count, median finish time, and fastest finish time that week. No filtering applied beyond the 22-year span (COVID gap 2020-03-14 to 2021-07-24 is marked but included).

Method. Extract the weekly fastest time (fastest_time_s) and median time (median_time_s) from the CSV. For each calendar year, compute the median of that year's weekly values. Plot both series alongside a background bar chart of weekly finisher counts. Overlay a shaded band showing the elite SD (mean fastest ± 1 SD across all 1,093 weeks).

Maths & stats. Fit yearly data to linear trends: fastest time slope = −0.68 s/yr (equivalent to −15s over 22 years, r = −0.34, weak); median time slope = +16.5 s/yr (equivalent to +363s = +6.1 min over 22 years, r = 0.945, strong). Elite distribution: median = 967s, SD = 41s, IQR = [944, 990s]. Yearly fastest values span only 944–1000s (5.8% spread), confirming flatness.

Robustness. The naive headline "more finishers → slower times" (Pearson r = 0.88) is driven by shared time trend; within-year (detrended) r = 0.21, partial r = 0.41. The apparent negative correlation between field size and elite speed (r = −0.28) is an artifact of small early fields with no elite runners present (r = −0.55 for <100 finishers) but vanishes in mature large fields (r = −0.18 for >500 finishers).

How this was made — data, maths & stats

Data. All 1,093 Bushy parkrun events (Oct 2004 – Jun 2026) from individual weekly results files and event dates from bushy_event_index.csv. Total of ~914,000 finishes. Dropped athlete_id 2214 and races with missing/unparseable times. Per event, extracted the fastest male and female finish times and runner names.

Method. Built a staircase by scanning chronologically through all events and recording each time a new course record (fastest-ever finish) was set for men and women separately. Tracked the exact date, runner name, and cumulative finish count at each record-breaking event. Extended the final record flat to the latest event date to show the frozen plateau.

Maths & stats. No statistical test — purely observational. The finding is the duration of the plateau: 14 years (Aug 2012 to Jun 2026) with 753,000 men's finishes and 784,000 women's finishes, zero faster than the standing records. Records = min(finish_time) per gender per event, accumulated into a monotonic staircase.

Robustness. Red-team verified the top finishers are genuine elite runners (Baddeley 93.7% age-grade, Mottram 92%; O'Sullivan an Olympian). The plateau is not survivorship bias — unique sub-16 min male runners peaked in 2018–19 and 2022–24 (38–45 per year) yet still nobody beat 2012. How this was made — data, maths & stats

Data. Bushy parkrun 2004–2025 (1,093 events, ~20 years). Each runner who appeared ≥50 times at Bushy (n=3,505). Times parsed as mm:ss, bounded 13–70 min, athlete_id 2214 dropped. Reconstructed each runner's ordered Bushy appearances by date (appearance 1, 2, …, up to 50), not global parkrun count.

Method. For each runner, subtract their own mean time from every appearance to create deviations. Then plot the average deviation at each appearance number (1–50). This kills survivorship bias (slow newcomers quitting) and field-composition effects because every point is relative to that same person. Also computed: fraction of each runner's career at which their personal best lands, and tested whether late PBs are real with a permutation null (shuffle each runner's times).

Maths & stats. Within-person mean deviation: dev(k) = mean over runners of [t(k) − mean(t)], where k is appearance number. PB-timing test: observed median fraction 0.57, permutation null median 0.53; observed last-quarter fraction 33%, null 28%. Null shows +4 percentage-point real excess beyond "more tries" artifact. Peak form ~run 28 at −29s; debut +79s.

Robustness. Survivorship of curve shape ruled out by fixing the same 3,505 people across all appearance numbers—same people, so the rise then re-decline arc is genuine, not composition. Late-PB signal is real by permutation test, not a mechanical artifact of more chances. Aging effect (upturn from run 28 to 50) disclosed and validated by dose response: juniors PB at 80% of career, seniors 60%, veterans 47%.

How this was made — data, maths & stats

Data. Bushy parkrun finish records (2004–2026) for a fixed cohort of 5,494 runners with ≥30 finishes each, providing a constant sample across all run-index bins to eliminate survivorship bias. Each run is flagged "New PB!" when it beats the runner's previous personal best at Bushy. Athlete 2214 excluded. Run-index is Bushy-local (within one runner's sequence), not global parkrun number.

Method. For each runner's sequence, track whether each run (run 2 through run 30) was marked "New PB!" in the achievement field. Calculate the observed PB rate as the fraction of finishers who set a PB at run k. Compare this to the null model: the probability that run k beats all prior runs if times were i.i.d. random, which equals exactly 1/k. The shaded gap between the curves isolates genuine fitness improvement from statistical chance.

Maths & stats. Observed rate = PB(k) / finishes(k) at each k. Null model = 1 / k (record-statistics baseline). Excess learning gain = obs(k) − 1/k. At run 2: obs = 69.4%, null = 50%, excess = +19.4%. At run 12: obs = 17.8%, null = 8.3%, excess = +9.5% (halved). At run 30: obs = 7.6%, null = 3.3%, excess = +4.3%.

Robustness. A fixed cohort of runners with ≥30 finishes shows the same decay curve as the full pool, ruling out survivorship bias. Sensitivity to cohort threshold (K = 15, 30, 60 runs) gives stable excess at run 2 (0.68–0.71) and run 12 (0.17–0.19). Caveat: run-index is Bushy-local, not global parkrun number; experienced arrivals dilute the apparent learning effect, making the excess a conservative lower bound.

How this was made — data, maths & stats

Data. Bushy parkrun, 2004–2025 (22 full years, ≥200 finishers each). Finish times from bushy_*.csv, parsed as mm:ss or h:mm:ss, filtered to 10–120 minutes. Dropped athlete_id 2214. Total 7,451 finisher records across 2,949 distinct athletes.

Method. For each year, we computed the median, 95th percentile (p95), and 99th percentile (p99) finish times. The "spread" is p95 − median. We then tested whether spread grew due to calendar time (cultural shift to walking) or field size (crowding) by regressing spread on year and field size, and computing partial correlations to isolate each effect.

Maths & stats. spread = p95 − median (via numpy.percentile). Linear regression: spread ~ year gives R² = 0.82; spread ~ field_size gives R² = 0.62. Partial correlations: ρ(spread, year | field_size) = +0.73 (strong); ρ(spread, field_size | year) = −0.09 (negligible). Holding field size ~1,200 constant, spread still grows from 11 to 21 min. Key numbers: p99 +80% (32.5→58.6 min), p95 +52%, spread doubled (9.4→20.1 min), share >45 min: 0%→11%.

Robustness. Main confound tested and ruled out: crowding. Though field size correlates with spread, partial correlations show year dominates (field size adds nothing to a year-only model). Repeat-walker artifact: broad population (2,949 distinct slow finishers, 2.5 visits each, not a handful). Used p95/p99 (robust percentiles), not raw max, so a single 89-min outlier cannot drive the trend.

Data. 1,093 Bushy events, 972k rows. Dropped athlete_id 2214 and blank finish times (55,996 / 5.8%). Gender: Male 60.9% / Female 39.1% (364 blank gender rows dropped). Position normalized to rank-fraction within each event's finishers.

Method. Binned finish position into 20 deciles (0=front, 100=back) within each event. Calculated female share per bin, pooled across early era (2004–2012) and recent era (2018–2026). For each year, computed per-event median female finish percentile, then averaged across events.

Maths & stats. Rank-fraction: frac = (rank + 0.5) / n. Female share per bin: F / (F + M) × 100. Median woman percentile: mean(median(frac[female]) × 100) per year. Early era 30.1% female vs recent 42.1%; median woman moved from 76th to 67th percentile (9 points forward in 17 years).

Who keeps showing up

How this was made — data, maths & stats

Data. All 1,093 Bushy parkrun event files (2004–2026), 916,562 rows. Excluded athlete_id 2214 and blanks. Sample: 121,137 unique athletes. Each athlete's "appearance count" = number of distinct Bushy events they ran (our proxy for lifetime loyalty to this venue).

Method. For each cohort (by debut year: 2004–2010, 2011–2015, 2016–2020, 2021–2024), we applied discrete Kaplan–Meier survival estimation. At each milestone run k, we counted the risk set (athletes with ≥k appearances) and identified "events" (quits at exactly k) as athletes with nap=k and exposure>k (had a fair chance to return). Separately, for Panel B we restricted to runners with ≥26 available events and computed year-by-year one-and-done rates.

Maths & stats. Discrete KM: S(k) = ∏(1 − q_i/R_i) where R(k) = #{nap ≥ k} and q(k) = #{nap = k AND expo > k}. Censoring correction: athletes with nap=k and expo≤k are excluded from the quit count (they had no real chance to return). Panel C verified the impact: at run 100, naive vs. KM differ only 1.49% vs. 1.48% — censoring is a null result because the dataset spans 22 years and 76.6% already have expo≥100.

Robustness. "Bushy loyalty" ≠ "parkrun loyalty": runners loyal to parkrun but at other venues count as churn here. This makes our estimates upper bounds on true quit rates. The secular decline (17% one-and-done in 2005 → 77.5% in 2024) survives the censoring correction and the fair-chance exposure gate (≥26 events), but we flag honestly that modern venue proliferation (~5 in 2007 → ~1,200 now) could partly explain the trend.

How this was made — data, maths & stats

Data. Per-athlete snapshot from Bushy parkrun event CSVs (2004–2026, 1,093 events). Each athlete's final lifetime parkrun total recorded at their last-ever Bushy appearance. Sample: 77,399 athletes whose last appearance was ≥2 years before the cutoff (ensuring they had genuinely quit, not censored mid-career).

Method. For each count from 1–200 lifetime runs, compute the observed frequency of athletes whose final run total was exactly that number. Calculate a local baseline by averaging counts of non-round numbers within a ±5-run window (e.g., the baseline for 50 uses 48,49,51,52 but excludes all multiples of 5). The ratio of observed to baseline reveals heaping above (magnet) or below (anti-magnet) the expected rate.

Maths & stats. ratio(n) = count(n) / mean(count of non-round neighbours). Bootstrap-like 95% CI via binomial variance. Key results: 25 runs ratio = 0.84 (16% fewer quit); 50 runs ratio = 1.18 (18% more); 100 runs ratio = 1.27 (27% more). Placebo control (non-shirt round numbers 30,40,60,70,75,80,120,150): average ratio = 1.006, confirming effect is milestone-specific.

Robustness. Tested across censoring windows (52/104/156 weeks). Bushy-dominant athletes (≥70% of runs at Bushy): effect stronger (50→1.43, 100→1.54), ruling out confound that athletes quit Bushy but ran elsewhere. The 25 anti-magnet result (opposite direction from 50/100) is genuine: the first shirt is a retention hook, but reaching 50 or 100 predicts exit.

How this was made — data, maths & stats

Data. All 1093 Bushy parkrun event files (data/bushy_*.csv), 2007–2025, excluding thin early years 2004–06 and partial 2026. Athlete ID 2214 dropped. Age derived from age_group field (e.g., SM25-29 → midpoint 27). Blank age rows (~6%) and wheelchair entries excluded. ~900k person-finishes analysed.

Method. For each year, parse event CSVs and extract athlete age from age_group band. Compute three means: (1) person-event mean (each finish counts once), (2) unique-person mean (each runner counted once per year), (3) debut mean (first-timers only, flagged by first appearance of athlete_id). Also tabulate finishers into age bands [under 35, 35-49, 50-59, 60+] and calculate share per year.

Maths & stats. Mean age = Σ(age) / n per year for each of three populations. Band share = (count in band / total) × 100%. Key findings: person-event mean rose 36.8 → 45.3 yr (+8.5); debut mean rose 33.1 → 39.8 yr (+6.7); 60+ share tripled 3.5% → 21.7%.

Robustness. Survivorship bias (cohort ageing in place) tested by plotting debut age separately — both field and newcomers rise in lockstep, ruling out fixed-cohort ageing as sole cause. Unique-person dedup (+6.8 yr) excludes repeat-attendance weighting. Caveat: first appearance in dataset approximates true debut; pre-2004 history unavailable, so early founders' true debuts before 2004 left-edge effect only.

How this was made — data, maths & stats

Data. All 1,093 Bushy parkrun results CSV files (2005–2026), with athlete_id, name, age_group, and gender. Excluded athlete_id 2214 and anonymous "Unknown" runners (5.8%). Extracted first name (first token) from each result; converted age_group to midpoint age (e.g., "25-29" → 27). Sample: 82–90% coverage per year for names with ≥200 bearers globally.

Method. Built a "name-age signature" by computing the global mean bearer age for each first name across all years (minimum 200 occurrences). For each year 2005–2026, calculated two parallel means: the actual mean finisher age, and the name-implied mean age (the average of the signature ages of that year's runners). Tracked both trends to reveal the divergence.

Maths & stats. For each name, signature_age(name) = mean(age_group_midpoint) where count(name) ≥ 200. Then per year, implied_age(year) = mean(signature_age(fname)) for all runs that year. Linear correlation: r(actual_age, implied_age) = −0.83 (2005–2026). Real age rose +6.5 years (38.8 → 45.7); implied age fell −4.2 years (46.4 → 42.1).

Robustness. Red-teamed four confounds: (1) Junior invasion — restricting to adult-coded names (mean bearer age ≥25), implied age still fell 48.1→43.8. (2) Female influx — effect holds within each sex (male 47.7→44.3, female 48.9→42.9). (3) Coverage drift — ≥200-bearer map stable at 82–90%. Method. For each unique athlete, extracted their maximum lifetime runs count across all event records. Maths & stats. Gini coefficient: G = (2 · Σ(i · x_i)) / (N · total) − (N+1)/N where x_i are sorted lifetime runs and i = 1…N. Robustness checks: Gini among athletes with ≥2 runs = 0.605; ≥5 runs = 0.547. Bottom 50% hold 4.63% of runs; top 1% hold 6.06%; top 10% hold 40.4%.

Data. Data. Bushy parkrun, 1,076 weekly events (seq 1–1093), eligible finishers >20 (non-first-timers, who cannot set PBs). For each week: finisher count, achievement flag ("New PB!"), and local weather (tmax, wind, precip) from Open-Meteo.

Method. The planet hits pause

How this was made — data, maths & stats

Data. Weekly totals of finishers summed across 13–146 sampled events per country, from parkrun event history CSV files, 2019-01-01 to 2023-12-31 (8 target countries: UK, USA, Australia, New Zealand, Ireland, South Africa, Poland, Germany). Each country's series is a sample, not a census.

Method. Dates are converted to ISO weeks (Monday-anchored). Finishers are summed by week and country. Each country is then indexed to its own 2019 baseline: the mean of all weekly totals falling within 2019 (=100). The cliff is identified as the first week in early 2020 where the index drops below 5 and stays below 5 for at least 4 consecutive weeks. Return is defined as the first week after the cliff where the index climbs back above 25 and the forward 4-week mean exceeds 25 (sustained reopening, not a brief spike).

Maths & stats. index = (weekly_finishers / 2019_baseline_mean) × 100. Cliff threshold: index < 5 for ≥4 weeks. Return threshold: index > 25 and mean(index[week:week+4]) > 25. Results: NZ returned in ~15 weeks (Jun 2020), South Africa in ~81 weeks (Jan 2022)—a 19-month spread.

Robustness. This is a sample index valid for shape and timing comparison, not absolute participation counts. Baseline values vary widely (UK 25.5k/wk vs Germany 90/wk) purely due to sample size and event age; indexing to 2019=100 makes cross-country comparison fair. Sawtooth dips after reopening (especially NZ and Australia) reflect regional lockdowns, not aggregate collapse.

How this was made — data, maths & stats

Data. Stratified sample of event histories (n=1,391 events with complete data in 2019 and 2023; full calendar years required). All 5K events; dropped the 3 events that truly ceased operating. Filtered to events with ≥20 weekly runs in each year (reducing survivor bias).

Method. For each event, computed recovery ratio = mean weekly finishers in 2023 ÷ mean weekly finishers in 2019 (full year averages). Maths & stats. Per-event ratio: r_i = Σ(finishers_2023) / n_wks ÷ Σ(finishers_2019) / n_wks (mean of weekly totals). Country-level statistic: median of all event ratios in that country. The fragile scaffolding

How this was made — data, maths & stats

Data. All rows from 111 parkrun event history files (databook/data/histories/*.csv), filtered to occurrences with finishers > 0 and volunteers > 0. Final sample: 41,429 valid occurrences across 111 events in four countries (UK, Australia, US, Denmark). Finishers ranged 1–6,204 (median ~150); volunteers 1–136 (median ~14).

Method. Per-country binned medians (model-free, robust to fitting): grouped occurrences by finisher count into bins (edges at 15, 30, 50, 80, 120, 180, 280, 450, 750, 1,300, 7,000), computed the median volunteers and finishers in each bin (bins with <25 occurrences dropped), then plotted the curve. Separately, fitted log-log OLS per country: ln(V) = ln(a) + α·ln(F).

Maths & stats. Power law V ≈ a·F^α, estimated by OLS on log-transformed data. Per-country α (95% CI): UK 0.44±0.01, US 0.44±0.01, Denmark 0.45±0.01, Australia 0.30±0.01. All decisively <1 (sublinear). Binned medians show volunteer floor varies culturally: UK ~10, US/Australia ~8, Denmark ~5 at low turnout; ratio falls from ~0.35 (tiny events) to ~0.04 (1,000+ finishers).

Robustness. Pooled (country-mixed) OLS drifts with crawl size (α =0.533 earlier, 0.481 now); per-country estimates and model-free binned medians are stable across snapshots. The sublinear signal holds in every country; confounds ruled out: event maturity and country launch dates overlap heavily across all four nations.

Showing up

parkrun is an astonishingly global, fragile, human act of showing up. The median participant does it exactly once — yet a devoted core has done it 500+ times, across a hundred countries, through a pandemic, in the rain. Every Saturday, the same ritual, repeated, is what makes the regularities in this book visible at all.
